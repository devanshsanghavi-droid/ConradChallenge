# ResidualMap

**One sentence:** a small water utility takes a handful of chlorine grab samples a month; ResidualMap turns them into a chlorine map of the whole pipe network with calibrated uncertainty, flags every junction likely to be below the 0.2 mg/L minimum, and tells the operator where to sample next.

Conrad Challenge 2026–27, Water Challenge. Software only. Working name — rename freely.

## The problem in one paragraph

Most of the ~50,000 US community water systems are small and run by one or two operators with no sensors and no SCADA. They keep the required minimum chlorine residual by grabbing a few samples and assuming the rest of the network looks the same. Every experiment in this repo shows that assumption flags **zero** low-chlorine nodes — because the average of a few samples sits above 0.2 mg/L while 10–25% of the network is under it at midday, and up to 40% at night.

## What the model does (iteration 3)

The operator's own EPANET model is the prior. EPANET already solves the transport physics exactly — mixing at junctions, tank turnover, travel time through every pipe with its real length, diameter and roughness. What it lacks is the decay chemistry and the honest size of its own hydraulic errors. ResidualMap calibrates the first from grab samples and models the second:

1. **Simulator grid.** Chlorine is simulated on the operator's model over a grid of the three decay unknowns — bulk decay `kb`, wall decay `kw`, and `gamma`, how much old (rough) pipe accelerates wall decay — times two hydraulic-mismatch axes, demand ×{0.85, 1, 1.15} and pipe roughness ×{0.9, 1, 1.1}. 675 EPANET runs, ~20 s on Net3, cached per network; all 24 hours of every run are kept. A source-dose axis ×{0.9 … 1.1} costs nothing: first-order decay is linear in concentration, so it is an exact offset in log space.
2. **Bayesian calibration.** Each grid member is weighted by how well it explains the samples (Student-t likelihood, scale 0.35 in ln C — the model error, not the grab-sample noise — so one junction where the operator's model is structurally wrong cannot hijack the fit). The weighted mean of log-chlorine is the mean function; the weighted spread is the parameter uncertainty that feeds the map's error bars.
3. **Local hydraulic error.** Calibrating the global demand and roughness multipliers does not remove the operator's per-node and per-pipe errors of the same size; the grid's own spread along the hydraulic axes at each (hour, junction) is carried into the band as their variance.
4. **Discrepancy GP.** A Matérn-ARD Gaussian process on water age, a hydraulic-distance embedding, the path wall-exposure index, distance to source and — in the time-aware model — the hour of day learns what the simulator still gets wrong.
5. **Outputs per junction:** median, 90% band, `P(residual < 0.2 mg/L)` at any hour; the daily minimum and `P(daily minimum < 0.2)` by Monte Carlo over grid members, local hydraulic deviations and joint 24-h GP draws.
6. **Next sample:** random, max-uncertainty, *straddle* (level-set estimation: sample where the model is least sure whether a node is above or below 0.2) and *straddle on the daily minimum* (pick the junction whose daily-minimum verdict is least sure, sample it at the daytime hour that constrains it most). Rules use the reducible part of the uncertainty (parameters + GP), not the local hydraulic term that no sample can shrink.

This is a Kennedy–O'Hagan calibration-plus-discrepancy model with a grid posterior instead of MCMC.

Three other surrogates are kept for comparison: the iteration-1 decay-law GP (first-order decay on water age as the mean), the same GP on the full 24-feature "deep dive" set (`docs/feature_dictionary.md`), and a graph physics-informed neural network (`residualmap/pinn.py`).

## Iteration 3 — daytime samples predict the night

The compliance number is the **daily minimum**, which happens at night; operators sample by day. `residualmap/simgp.py::SimGP24` takes samples as (junction, hour, mg/L), scores every grid member on the simulated value at each sample's own hour, gives the discrepancy GP the hour (sin/cos) and the water age at that hour — with a length-scale floor of about three hours, because a discrepancy driven by the diurnal demand pattern cannot change in fifteen minutes — and predicts the whole 24-h profile at every junction. The daily minimum and `P(daily min < 0.2)` come from Monte-Carlo draws (grid member and dose by weight, a random local hydraulic deviation, a joint 24-h GP draw per junction; take the minimum). Sampling hours are restricted to 07:00–17:00.

## A monthly route from the file alone (`outputs/route_comparison_Net3.png`)

Operators drive a fixed route, not one sample at a time. `residualmap/route.py::plan_route` picks K (junction, hour) sites at once from the EPANET file before any sample exists — greedily on the daily-minimum straddle score with a hydraulic-distance repulsion so the sites spread over the network like a random route would, at most ⌈K/11⌉ sites per daytime hour so one person can drive it — and gives a one-sentence reason per site. ## Water age, and where chlorine is lost (iteration 4, task 9; `outputs/chem/water_age_Net3.png`)

Water age was always inside the model (EPANET moves the water through every pipe and tank, and the age at each hour is an input of the discrepancy GP); now the operator sees it. The app maps the water age of the operator's file and how far it moves across the grid's 9 demand and roughness settings, and splits each junction's chlorine loss between the water itself and the pipe walls for the decay rates the samples calibrate. All of it is tested against a simulated truth only (`python -m residualmap.experiment_chem age <net> <seeds>`; default truth, pooled over seeds; numbers from `outputs/chem/water_age_<net>.json` and `water_age_junctions_<net>.csv`).

Every age is a 7-day-run age. Each run starts with the file's water in the pipes and tanks, and where some of it is still arriving on the scored day the age is a lower bound: the water is older than the run can show. On the day's average that is 19 of Net3's 92 junctions, 28 of Net2's 35 and 745 of ky4's 959 (at their oldest hour, 87, 29 and 799), and the app says "at least" there. The wall-versus-water split is exact along a single path from the source and within 5% of additive where flows mix on Net3 and ky4 under the default truth; walls dominate in these synthetic truths, which is a property of the truths, not a field result. ## Water temperature (iteration 4, task 10): tested out of season, not adopted (`outputs/chem/summer_forecast_Net3.png`)

Chlorine decays faster in warm water. Today's model handles that only by refitting its decay rates from the last three months of samples. Task 10 built the alternative and tested it against bars written down before the first scored run (`docs/iteration3_journal.md`, task 10): a plant log of monthly water temperature (`python -m residualmap.pilot ... --plant plant_log.csv`, format in `docs/example_plant_log.csv`) and a model (`residualmap/seasonal.py::SeasonalSimGP24`) that explains every grab sample at its own month's temperature and predicts any month at its own. The test: 12 simulated months on Net3 (16 seeds) and Net2 (8), against a hidden truth with its own temperature sensitivity, a 15 C control, and water warming in the pipes that the model cannot see. The temperatures are an assumed coastal schedule, not measurements (`python -m residualmap.seasonal <net> <seeds>`; numbers from `outputs/chem/summary_season_<net>.json`).

The cause is mostly the pipe walls. ## Organics, TOC (iteration 4, task 11): tested, stopped at its stop rule, not adopted (`outputs/chem/first_storm_Net3.png`)

Organic matter in the water consumes chlorine, and many surface waters carry more of it after the first winter storms. Today's model absorbs that into the bulk decay rate it refits from the last three months of samples, so a model fitted on a dry late summer cannot know that November's water will use up chlorine faster. Task 11 makes the plant's monthly TOC an input: `residualmap/organics.py::TocSimGP24` (M_TOC) keeps today's 675-member grid as one hypothesis and adds the same grid with every bulk decay rate times TOC / 2.0 mg/L, the TOC-proportional form fitted by AWWARF and by Powell et al. That is likely because its map is biased low (-0.043 against -0.036 mg/L at 8 random samples) and the two-reactant truth has more junction-days just above 0.2 mg/L (84 against 53 from 0.20 to 0.25 mg/L, counted after the review); how much is the bias and how much the kinetics was not separated (journal, What needs a decision, c).
- **Recall.** One measurable loss inside the 10%: on Net2 at 15 random samples, 67 of 77 low junction-days found against 93 of 100 (ratio 0.936, interval 0.847 to 0.991).
- **Dose step (an expectation, held).** Moving a map fitted at 1.2 mg/L to a new plant dose by the exact first-order offset was conservative going up (0.021 mg/L lower than on the first-order twin at 1.6 mg/L) and optimistic going down (0.006 higher at 0.9 mg/L).

With today's model's most probable bulk rate at its grid floor of 0.10 per day in 45% (Net3) and 96% (Net2) of its fits on the two-reactant truth, an opt-in grid with four lower bulk rates (0.0125 to 0.075 per day, its own cache file) was tested as pre-registered. It met all three of its readings: no loss beyond 10% on the first-order truths, a 90% band in range on every truth, and measurable RMSE gains on the two-reactant truth (for example 0.881 times today's at 15 random samples on Net3). It is a candidate for the default grid, but adopting it would move every committed number, so that is Devansh's decision; it would also trade some recall for fewer false alarms (Net3, first-order truth, 8 random samples: recall 0.926 against 0.981, false alarms 18 against 32). On Net2 its own new floor still holds the most probable rate in about half of the two-reactant fits. The default model and every committed number are unchanged.

## Honest limitations

- **Structural mismatch is tested, not represented.** The grid covers global demand, roughness and dose errors and the band carries local ones; a closed pipe or a wrong tank volume is absorbed (recall holds, see the stress test) but never identified. Finding *which* pipe is closed from chlorine samples is a different, later problem.
- **The band is honest on average, not at every n.** Snapshot: 0.95 for a 90% band over n = 3–15, but the 50% band over-covers (0.68) — slightly too wide in the middle. Daily minimum: 0.93 on average, narrowing from 0.96 at n=3 to 0.88 at n=15 (0.84 with the straddle-on-daily-minimum rule). Treat `P(daily min < 0.2)` from a straddle-chosen set of 15 samples as slightly over-confident.
- **Recall traded for precision on the 14:00 snapshot.** The better-calibrated posterior shrinks the low junctions' medians toward the grid centre: recall under random samples is about 5% lower than with the iteration-2 grid (precision 10–16 points higher, F1 higher). The max-uncertainty rule lost 20% recall and should not be used.
- **The daily-minimum recall is nearly saturated on Net3.** Any 5 daytime samples find 97% or more of the junctions that go below 0.2 mg/L at night, because a whole tank-fed zone goes low together. Route and rule comparisons on that metric are decided by two hard scenarios out of eight; the 14:00 snapshot (recall 0.74–0.92) and the 0.2 mg/L precision are the discriminating numbers. Larger networks (task 5) should separate the routes more.
- **Synthetic truth.** No real grab-sample data yet. `docs/pilot_protocol.md` is the path to it: what a utility gives us, how taps become junctions, the monthly deliverable, and the hold-out validation (`python -m residualmap.pilot`) with success criteria fixed in advance — including rotating off-route sites, because a fixed route can never validate the map.
- **Grid posterior is coarse** (5×5×3 decay × 3×3 hydraulic × 5 dose). Fine for six parameters with a 20-second cache; MCMC or an emulator if more are added.
- **The discrepancy GP does not scale to 959 junctions at 15 samples.** On ky4 it raises the snapshot RMSE from 0.08 to 0.11 and the straddle rule's to 0.21; the calibrated simulator with its band is the better map there. A rule for when to switch the GP on (n ≥ J/25, or by marginal likelihood) is the next iteration's job. The 675-run grid takes 15 minutes and 62 MB on ky4.
- **Water chemistry is reduced to calibrated bulk and wall decay rates per three-month window, with chloramine as a separate mode (iteration 4, tasks 8 to 14).** Water age is handled exactly, through EPANET's transport in every simulation, and since task 9 it is shown. Water temperature and organics (TOC) are not inputs: their effect is absorbed into the bulk and wall decay rates, which are refitted from the last three months of samples, so a model fitted in winter has no way to know that summer water decays faster. Iteration 4 tested temperature-aware and TOC-aware models in simulation against chemistry the model does not assume and adopted neither (below). Chloraminated systems have a separate chloramine mode since task 12 (below). All seven tasks are done: the plumbing (`residualmap/chemistry.py`), the saved checks (`python -m residualmap.checks`, 69 of 69 passing: 68 in `outputs/chem/checks_report.json`, and a names check that needs a private list and is kept in the git-ignored run log), water age made visible with the wall-versus-water split of the chlorine loss (above), two temperature-aware models, the second of which learns the pipe wall's temperature response, both tested out of season and stopped at their pre-registered stop rules, a TOC-aware model tested against second-order chlorine-organics chemistry and stopped at its stop rule too, a chloramine mode tested against EPA's chloramine chemistry in EPANET-MSX, which met its pre-registered bars where they apply and is an explicit choice in the app and the pilot path (above), an audit of today's first-order model against two-reactant chlorine-organics chemistry (above), and the app's 'What this model accounts for' table with the PDF's chemistry lines (task 14). They leave the Net3, Net2, persistent stress-test and synthetic-pilot outputs unchanged, 50 of 50 files byte-identical (`outputs/chem/baseline_reproduction.json`); ky4 and the initial-level structural variant were not rerun. Every iteration-4 number in this README and the journal is checked against `outputs/` by `python -m residualmap.numbers`, which shows that a number exists in a committed output file and, for task 14's headline numbers, that it is the value at the named key; for the rest it cannot show which key a number came from (journal, task 14).
- **Temperature is tested, not adopted** (tasks 10 and 10b). In simulation task 10's temperature-aware model gave no measurable gain on held-out readings. Fitted in winter and asked for summer it was worse; fitted in summer and asked for December it had the better map RMSE but lower recall. The main cause is that it assumed pipe-wall decay rises with temperature as steeply as bulk decay. How real walls respond is not known, and the bulk-only variant that did better on Net3 was not the pre-registered model and would also have triggered the stop rule on Net2. Its temperatures (10 to 20 C), the soil curve and the truth's wall response are assumptions; seasonal demand is not simulated; ky4 was not tested (the plan estimates about 5 hours for one bank there; not measured). Task 10b's model, which learns the wall's response, gained on held-out readings in every simulated world but triggered its stop rule in three of four network and world pairs, so it is not adopted either.
- **Organics (TOC) are tested, not adopted** (task 11). In simulation at a fixed 20 C, a model told the plant's monthly TOC predicted held-out readings measurably better than today's, and its map was measurably better after the TOC fell (part of the gain, by a grid-only control after the review, came from its wider bulk-rate grid and not from TOC), but at the first storms (TOC doubling in November, an assumed schedule) its map RMSE was 0.938 of today's (interval 0.874 to 1.016, not a measurable gain) against a pre-registered 0.85, in December its map was worse (the low bias below, which today's TOC-blind error partly cancels), and its stop rule triggered (one robust reading-recall pair on Net3, three within count noise on Net2). The truths' kinetic constants and the TOC schedule are assumptions; one limiting concentration per run assumes one plant dose; ky4 was not tested. Today's daily-minimum map is biased low by about 0.05 mg/L on Net3 and 0.03 on Net2 in these 12-month simulations (and in task 9's), which is conservative for recall and was not changed. Today's bulk-rate grid floor (0.10 per day) binds in most fits under the second-order truths and on Net2; that was not changed either.
- **The chloramine mode is tested in simulation only** (task 12). It met its pre-registered bars where they apply against EPA's chloramine chemistry on Net3 and Net2, but its truth is EPA's best case (constant pH, organic-matter constants at 25 C) plus this task's own wall and nitrification terms. Monochloramine's wall diffusivity, the wall-rate grid and the prior's spread are assumptions. A blend of chloraminated and chlorinated water can cross breakpoint, which no single-species model represents. Total chlorine includes organic chloramines with little disinfecting power. Under the straddle rule the band is too narrow (0.73 at 8 samples on Net3), and at 15 straddle samples on Net3 the free-chlorine settings had the lower RMSE. On Net2 the random-sample coverage is inside its band only at 8 samples (0.980 at 3, 0.806 at 15). Its prior assumes the plant's pH and Cl2:N are logged without error. No seasonal chloramine test was run, and ky4 was not tested. The nitrification watch is a rule with literature thresholds, not a model of nitrification.
- **First-order chemistry is audited, not replaced** (task 13). Against Fisher's two-reactant chemistry at a fixed 20 C, the pre-registered verdict is that first order's cost is material, from one cell of 16 inside seed noise (Net2, 15 straddle samples, RMSE 1.102 times), and the two-rate model that gate built was rejected for losing more than 10% on the first-order truth; in the other 15 cells today's daily-minimum RMSE and recall stayed within 10%. The oldest-water bar triggered on Net3: a map 0.076 mg/L too low in its oldest tenth of junctions (0.031 on the first-order truth; 0.045 attributable to the chemistry, inside the bar), recorded as first order's limit, with the next step (a second-order grid with a simulated dose axis) named, not built. With no bar, false alarms about doubled on Net3. The two-reactant constants come from one water via a secondary source, its organic loads were tuned to match the committed decay rate at 24 h, it runs at one temperature and one TOC, ky4 was not run, and the low-bulk-rate grid that did better there is opt-in and not the default.
- **The water-age range is not called a 90% band** (task 9): it covers the simulated truth's daily-mean age at 0.58 to 0.87 of junctions (0.18 on Net2 with a wrong tank) and the daily-max age at 0.78 to 0.81, because errors in single junctions' demands and single pipes' roughness are not among its 9 global settings. ky4's daily-mean 0.868 would pass the plan's 0.85 bar; the label rule here is stricter. The age weighted by the samples' posterior is closer on average but its band is narrower still. The calibrated split puts less of the loss at the walls than the truth on Net2 (median 0.768 against 0.818) and ky4 (0.538 against 0.624).
- **Every 7-day run carries a start-up transient** (found in task 9, not fixed). Runs start the file's tanks and pipes with no chlorine and with water of age 0, and on the scored day the median junction's water is still 13.7% initial contents on Net2 and 15.9% on ky4 (1.3% on Net3; `outputs/chem/water_age_<net>.json`), with some ky4 junctions reached by no source water in 7 days. So the water ages are lower bounds at most Net2 and ky4 junctions (above), and the truth and the model's grid start alike, so model-against-truth scores are consistent, but absolute counts of low junctions on Net2 and ky4 partly reflect it. A longer warm-up, or tanks started at source quality, is the proposed fix.
- **The route optimiser's edge is Net3-specific so far.** On Net2 and ky4 the K highest-demand sites do as well or better on recall, and the straddle-chosen sites hurt calibration at K=12. A mixed objective (half the sites for the violation call, half to calibrate) is proposed in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## The app (`app.py`)

```bash
streamlit run app.py
```

Pick an example network or upload your `.inp`, set the plant dose, type your grab samples as rows (junction, hour, mg/L), and read four panels: the chlorine map with your samples marked, the 90% band, `P(residual < 0.2)` at the daily minimum or at any hour, and next month's route with a one-sentence reason per site. Below them, the water age of your file with its range across demand and roughness errors (marked "at least" where the 7-day simulation has not replaced the water it started with), and where each junction's chlorine is lost (water or pipe walls) for the single most likely decay rates your samples calibrate; the route reasons and the PDF carry the water age too. Download the one-page PDF. With no samples yet the map is your model's physics alone. Choose "Chloramine (total chlorine)" under Disinfectant for a chloraminated system: the readings are total chlorine, and the model switches to the chloramine mode's grid and prior (from your plant's logged pH and chlorine-to-ammonia ratio; in demo mode the hidden truth's own values, which makes the demo slightly optimistic). The minimum residual defaults to 0.5 mg/L total chlorine, labelled as a common utility operating target, not a California rule. A nitrification watch panel appears, labelled as literature thresholds, and in demo mode the hidden truth comes from EPA's chloramine chemistry in EPANET-MSX (about 15 s, run in a separate process; minutes without a C compiler). Demo mode simulates a hidden truth from the same file, draws daytime samples from it, and can reveal the truth to score the flags. Runs on a laptop; nothing leaves it.

Below the panels, 'What this model accounts for' (iteration 4, task 14) answers water age, organics and chlorine demand, chlorine type and temperature in one row each: what the model does, how it was tested in simulation (the numbers are read from `outputs/` when the app runs, through `residualmap/capabilities.py`), and what it does not do or what was tested and not adopted. The PDF names the disinfectant and the measured species, the minimum residual and its source, says that water temperature and TOC are absorbed into the decay rates refitted every three months, and says 'Validated in simulation only'. A warning appears when more than half of the calibration sits on the lowest or highest decay rate of the grid. If EPANET-MSX cannot run, the chloramine demo falls back to a reduced first-order truth and says that this flatters the model. Task 14 also fixed a fault from iteration 3: the app could not build a grid for an uploaded file or an uncached network, because each worker of the grid build re-ran the whole app first (journal, task 14).
