# ResidualMap

**One sentence:** a small water utility takes a handful of chlorine grab samples a month; ResidualMap turns them into a chlorine map of the whole pipe network with calibrated uncertainty, flags every junction likely to be below the 0.2 mg/L minimum, and tells the operator where to sample next.

Conrad Challenge 2026–27, Water Challenge. Software only. Working name — rename freely.

## The problem in one paragraph

Most of the ~50,000 US community water systems are small and run by one or two operators with no sensors and no SCADA. They keep the required minimum chlorine residual by grabbing a few samples and assuming the rest of the network looks the same. Every experiment in this repo shows that assumption flags **zero** low-chlorine nodes — because the average of a few samples sits above 0.2 mg/L while 10–25% of the network is under it at midday, and up to 40% at night.

## What the model does (iteration 3)

The operator's own EPANET model is the prior. EPANET already solves the transport physics exactly — mixing at junctions, tank turnover, travel time through every pipe with its real length, diameter and roughness. What it lacks is the decay chemistry and the honest size of its own hydraulic errors. ResidualMap calibrates the first from grab samples and models the second:

1. **Simulator grid.** Chlorine is simulated on the operator's model over a grid of the three decay unknowns — bulk decay `kb`, wall decay `kw`, and `gamma`, how much old (rough) pipe accelerates wall decay — times two hydraulic-mismatch axes, demand ×{0.85, 1, 1.15} and pipe roughness ×{0.9, 1, 1.1}. 675 EPANET runs, ~20 s on Net3, cached per network; all 24 hours of every run are kept. A source-dose axis ×{0.9 … 1.1} costs nothing: first-order decay is linear in concentration, so it is an exact offset in log space.
2. **Bayesian calibration.** Each grid member is weighted by how well it explains the samples (Student-t likelihood, scale 0.35 in ln C — the model error, not the grab-sample noise — so one junction where the operator's model is structurally wrong cannot hijack the fit). The weighted mean of log-chlorine is the mean function; the weighted spread is the parameter uncertainty that feeds the map's error bars.
3. **Local hydraulic error.** Calibrating the global demand and roughness multipliers does not remove the operator's per-node and per-pipe errors of the same size; the grid's own spread along the hydraulic axes at each (hour, junction) is carried into the band as their variance.
4. **Discrepancy GP.** A Matérn-ARD Gaussian process on water age, a hydraulic-distance embedding, the path wall-exposure index, distance to source and — in the time-aware model — the hour of day learns what the simulator still gets wrong.
5. **Outputs per junction:** median, 90% band, `P(residual < 0.2 mg/L)` at any hour; the daily minimum and `P(daily minimum < 0.2)` by Monte Carlo over grid members, local hydraulic deviations and joint 24-h GP draws.
6. **Next sample:** random, max-uncertainty, *straddle* (level-set estimation: sample where the model is least sure whether a node is above or below 0.2) and *straddle on the daily minimum* (pick the junction whose daily-minimum verdict is least sure, sample it at the daytime hour that constrains it most). Rules use the reducible part of the uncertainty (parameters + GP), not the local hydraulic term that no sample can shrink.

This is a Kennedy–O'Hagan calibration-plus-discrepancy model with a grid posterior instead of MCMC.

Three other surrogates are kept for comparison: the iteration-1 decay-law GP (first-order decay on water age as the mean), the same GP on the full 24-feature "deep dive" set (`docs/feature_dictionary.md`), and a graph physics-informed neural network (`residualmap/pinn.py`).

**What the numbers say**

- **Three grab samples plus the calibrated simulator beat every other model at fifteen.** RMSE 0.12 mg/L at n=3 vs 0.17 for the iteration-1 GP at n=15.
- **Nine in ten of the low-chlorine junctions are found with eight samples chosen by the straddle rule** (recall 0.93, F1 0.94); random samples find 0.74 at three and 0.84 at fifteen.
- **The band is honest now.** Iteration 2 under-covered (0.75 for a 90% band); with demand, roughness and dose on the grid and the local hydraulic error carried into the band, coverage is on the diagonal at 80–95% and slightly wide at 50%.
- **The calibration recovers the physics.** Averaged over seeds, the posterior mode lands at `kw` ≈ 0.75 m/day and `gamma` ≈ 0.94 — the truth was 0.7 and 1.0. From eight grab samples the model works out that old pipes are eating the chlorine.
- **More features did not help; more physics did.** The 24-feature "deep dive" set slightly hurts the decay-law GP at n ≥ 12 (over-fitting) and is a wash or worse for the simulator GP. Physics pays off when it enters through the simulator, not the feature list. The features remain useful for explaining *why* a node is low.
- **PINN verdict.** The graph-PINN (first-order decay enforced along every steadily-directed pipe, trained on all nodes' features with the sample labels) beats the decay-law GP on RMSE at n ≥ 10 but is far worse at finding violations (recall 0.05–0.38), is badly over-confident (coverage 0.37), and is 10× slower. At 3–15 samples a neural network has nothing to learn from that EPANET does not already compute. A PINN would earn its place when months of samples exist and the target is the pipe-level decay field.
- **The max-uncertainty rule is no longer useful** (recall 0.70 at n=15, was 0.90): it spends its samples on the violators themselves and then flags only 70% of the harder ones left; use the straddle rule.

**The finding that drives the pitch — `outputs/day_vs_night_Net3.png`:** in scenario 0, 11 of 92 junctions are below 0.2 mg/L at 14:00; 31 are at 22:00, 40% at midnight. Operators sample in the day. The network is worst at night.

## Iteration 3 — daytime samples predict the night

The compliance number is the **daily minimum**, which happens at night; operators sample by day. `residualmap/simgp.py::SimGP24` takes samples as (junction, hour, mg/L), scores every grid member on the simulated value at each sample's own hour, gives the discrepancy GP the hour (sin/cos) and the water age at that hour — with a length-scale floor of about three hours, because a discrepancy driven by the diurnal demand pattern cannot change in fifteen minutes — and predicts the whole 24-h profile at every junction. The daily minimum and `P(daily min < 0.2)` come from Monte-Carlo draws (grid member and dose by weight, a random local hydraulic deviation, a joint 24-h GP draw per junction; take the minimum). Sampling hours are restricted to 07:00–17:00.

## A monthly route from the file alone (`outputs/route_comparison_Net3.png`)

Operators drive a fixed route, not one sample at a time. `residualmap/route.py::plan_route` picks K (junction, hour) sites at once from the EPANET file before any sample exists — greedily on the daily-minimum straddle score with a hydraulic-distance repulsion so the sites spread over the network like a random route would, at most ⌈K/11⌉ sites per daytime hour so one person can drive it — and gives a one-sentence reason per site. Net3, 8 scenario-months, night violations **missed** at unsampled junctions, summed over the months (optimised route / K highest-demand sites / K random sites): **K=5: 4 / 9 / 3; K=8: 7 / 10 / 9; K=12: 2 / 8 / 6.** Recall 0.986 / 0.972 / 0.989, 0.972 / 0.968 / 0.969, 0.991 / 0.974 / 0.980; F1 of the flags highest for the optimised route at every K (0.942, 0.941, 0.949). The optimised route beats the highest-demand sites at every K and is within noise of random sites (3 misses behind at K=5, 2 and 4 ahead at K=8 and K=12). Four to six of the eight scenarios are recalled perfectly by every route, so the differences live in a few hard months. The straddle-chosen sites are the borderline, least-well-modelled junctions, so the route buys flags with map accuracy (daily-minimum RMSE 0.07–0.09 vs 0.07–0.08 for the highest-demand sites; coverage 0.83–0.90 vs 0.90–0.98).

## Stress test — the operator's file is structurally wrong (`outputs/stress_test_Net3.png`)

Real EPANET files have closed valves that are open, missing pipes, wrong tank data. `build_scenario(structural_noise="persistent")` gives the truth a closed non-bridge pipe (probability 0.5 per scenario; 2 of 8 drew one) and one tank with half the volume the file says (every scenario), moving the true chlorine by up to 0.5–0.8 mg/L at 26–77 junctions. Same model, same samples, Net3, 8 seeds, file correct → file wrong: 14:00 snapshot with the straddle rule, recall at 15 samples 0.92 → 0.92 (F1 0.93 → 0.95), random samples 0.84 → 0.80; 90% coverage 0.95 → 0.94. Daily minimum from daytime samples: recall 1.00 → 1.00, coverage 0.89 → 0.84. The discrepancy GP is worth about +5 recall and +11 coverage points at 15 samples over the calibrated simulator alone, wrong file or not; most of the robustness comes from the hydraulic axes and the local-hydraulic term in the band. The literal version of the test — a tank's *initial* level ×0.7 — changes the scored day by < 0.05 mg/L because the 7-day warm-up forgets it; both variants are in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Water age, and where chlorine is lost (iteration 4, task 9; `outputs/chem/water_age_Net3.png`)

Water age was always inside the model (EPANET moves the water through every pipe and tank, and the age at each hour is an input of the discrepancy GP); now the operator sees it. The app maps the water age of the operator's file and how far it moves across the grid's 9 demand and roughness settings, and splits each junction's chlorine loss between the water itself and the pipe walls for the decay rates the samples calibrate. All of it is tested against a simulated truth only (`python -m residualmap.experiment_chem age <net> <seeds>`; default truth, pooled over seeds; numbers from `outputs/chem/water_age_<net>.json` and `water_age_junctions_<net>.csv`).

Every age is a 7-day-run age. Each run starts with the file's water in the pipes and tanks, and where some of it is still arriving on the scored day the age is a lower bound: the water is older than the run can show. On the day's average that is 19 of Net3's 92 junctions, 28 of Net2's 35 and 745 of ky4's 959 (at their oldest hour, 87, 29 and 799), and the app says "at least" there. The errors are large next to the ages themselves: on Net3 the RMSE is more than half the median junction's age (10.50 h), and per seed it runs from 2.03 to 10.27 h. The range is called a "range across demand and roughness errors", not a 90% band, on all three networks, and the app quotes its coverage. ky4's daily-mean coverage (0.868) passes the plan's bar of 0.85, but its daily-max coverage (0.811), the age the app maps, does not; the rule used here asks every coverage in the file to reach 0.85, which is stricter than the plan. The cause is that the truth's per-junction demand and per-pipe roughness errors are not among the 9 global settings. Eight chlorine samples sharpen the pooled age on Net3 and ky4, but are worse than the file's own age on 2 of 8 seeds on Net3 and on Net2, and cannot see a wrong tank (Net2's stress test, most of it at junctions whose ages are lower bounds). The wall-versus-water split is exact along a single path from the source and within 5% of additive where flows mix on Net3 and ky4 under the default truth; walls dominate in these synthetic truths, which is a property of the truths, not a field result. The calibrated split's median is biased low on Net2 and ky4 and was off by up to 0.265 in a single Net2 scenario, so the app calls it an estimate. `outputs/chem/error_by_age_<net>.csv` gives the daily-minimum chlorine error by water-age decile (on Net3 both misses and 19 of 31 false alarms at 8 samples sit in the middle third of ages, where junctions cross 0.2 mg/L): the baseline for the chemistry audit in task 13.

## Water temperature (iteration 4, task 10): tested out of season, not adopted (`outputs/chem/summer_forecast_Net3.png`)

Chlorine decays faster in warm water. Today's model handles that only by refitting its decay rates from the last three months of samples. Task 10 built the alternative and tested it against bars written down before the first scored run (`docs/iteration3_journal.md`, task 10): a plant log of monthly water temperature (`python -m residualmap.pilot ... --plant plant_log.csv`, format in `docs/example_plant_log.csv`) and a model (`residualmap/seasonal.py::SeasonalSimGP24`) that explains every grab sample at its own month's temperature and predicts any month at its own. It keeps today's 675-member grid as one hypothesis and adds the same grid re-run under Arrhenius temperature laws (E/R 5000, 8000 and 12000 K, 1.8 to 4.2 times faster decay per 10 C), so at 20 C it is today's model exactly. The test: 12 simulated months on Net3 (16 seeds) and Net2 (8), against a hidden truth with its own temperature sensitivity, a 15 C control, and water warming in the pipes that the model cannot see. The temperatures are an assumed coastal schedule, not measurements (`python -m residualmap.seasonal <net> <seeds>`; numbers from `outputs/chem/summary_season_<net>.json`).

|---|---|---|---|
| Net3 (16) | 0.109 / 0.112 (0.974, 0.937 to 1.013): no measurable gain on Net3 | 0.137 / 0.101 mg/L; 0.996 / 0.753; 554 / 18 | triggered |
| Net2 (8) | 0.126 / 0.125 (1.007, 0.957 to 1.066): no measurable gain on Net2 | 0.134 / 0.095 mg/L; 1.000 / 0.494; 171 / 10 | triggered |

The temperature-aware model stopped at its own stop rule (A7), so it is not the default, and task 11 (below) went ahead without it. Two kinds of trigger carry the stop; their paired bootstrap 90% intervals over seeds lie wholly on the model's worse side of 1.0. On Net3, in the warming months, its daily-minimum map RMSE is 1.229 times today's under the seasonal truth (interval 1.112 to 1.352) and 1.208 times with water warming in the pipes (1.079 to 1.348). On Net2, in the cooling months, its map finds fewer low junctions: recall 0.813 of today's (100 against 123 of 140 low junction-months, interval 0.760 to 0.866), and 0.823 with warming in the pipes (121 against 147 of 157, 0.753 to 0.884). Net3's cooling-month reading recall (0.853 and 0.849 of today's, on 99 and 107 low readings) also excludes 1.0, but its interval reaches above the 0.90 bar. The other four Net2 triggers are within count noise, with intervals that reach 1.0: the warming-month map RMSE (1.134, 0.971 to 1.335) and three reading-recall ratios on 14 to 31 low readings, one of them in the 15 C control, where temperature cannot be the cause (5 against 7 of 31). Every trigger's counts and interval are in `outputs/chem/task10_mechanism_checks.json` (key `a7_triggers`).

The cause is mostly the pipe walls. The model assumed wall decay speeds up with temperature as steeply as decay in the water; in the simulated truth the wall responds much more weakly (1.0 to 1.8 times per 10 C against the model's 1.8 to 4.2), and on these networks most chlorine is lost at the walls, so the model expects summer to be far worse than it is. Two direct checks, run after the stop with the same code on Net3 (`python -m residualmap.seasonal Net3 8 --mechanism`, written to `outputs/chem/task10_mechanism_checks.json`): in a world where the truth's wall follows the model's assumption, and nothing else changes, the model beats today's out of season (fitted on January to March, July-to-September map RMSE 0.092 against 0.185 mg/L over 8 seeds); and on seed 0, giving the model the truth's own wall law cuts its July map RMSE from 0.154 to 0.098 mg/L. That is still worse than today's 0.082 on that seed, so the wall is not the whole story: E/R is not identified, and even with the right wall 0.42 of the posterior sits on the steepest law, E/R 12000 K, against the truth's 8581 K (journal, task 10, finding 2). The variant that leaves the wall chemistry at 20 C did better on Net3 (July-to-September map RMSE 0.077 against today's 0.101), but on Net2 it would also have triggered the stop rule (reading recall 0.800 to 0.889 of today's, on 14 to 31 low readings). It was an ablation read after the fact, not the pre-registered model, and nobody has measured how real pipe walls respond to temperature (the truth's weak wall is an assumption too).

What does work, on Net3: the model reproduces today's at a constant temperature (15 C control: RMSE ratios 0.988 and 0.982), its temperature-normalised bulk rate stays flat over the year where today's refit rides with the seasons (`outputs/chem/kb_by_month_Net3.png`), and a 2 C error in the logged temperature costs at most 0.007 of recall (0.013 on Net2). The control and the flat rate do not carry over to Net2, where the 15 C control found 7 low readings against today's 9 of 44 (a recall gap of 0.045 against the 0.03 bar) and the flat-rate bar failed because today's rate sits at the grid's floor all year. The 2 C result passes only because out-of-season recall is already near 1; the error moves false alarms instead (+2 C in the forecast month: 741 against 554 on Net3). In all, the model missed A4 on Net3 and A2, A3 and A5 on Net2, and triggered A7 on both.

### Task 10b: the model learns the wall's temperature response (tested, stopped, not adopted; `outputs/chem/season2_Net3.png`)

Task 10's model failed mostly because it assumed the pipe wall's chlorine decay speeds up with temperature as steeply as decay in the water. Task 10b lets the samples decide instead: model M2 (`residualmap/seasonal.py::WallSeasonalSimGP24`) crosses the three bulk temperature laws with five wall laws (E/R 0, 2500, 5000, 8000 and 12000 K, from no wall response to 4.2 times faster per 10 C), with task 10's model and its bulk-only variant nested inside it and their cached grids reused. It was tested against bars written before the first scored run (`docs/iteration3_journal.md`, task 10b), on fresh seeds (Net3 16 to 31, Net2 8 to 15), in two simulated worlds that differ only in the truth's wall: W1, task 10's weak wall (an assumption), and W2, a wall that follows the truth's own bulk law (`python -m residualmap.seasonal <net> <seeds> --wall`; numbers from `outputs/chem/summary_season2_<net>.json`).

But the stop rule triggered in three of the four network and world pairs, so it is not adopted. On Net3 in the weak-wall world it still expects summer water to lose more chlorine at the walls than it does (warming-month map RMSE 1.124 times today's, interval 1.056 to 1.191; 272 false alarms against 142), and, fitted on July to September, it flagged 8 of the 12 low December readings where today's model flagged all 12 (recall ratio 0.667, interval 0.400 to 0.889, the one trigger whose interval lies wholly past the bar; 6 of 7 in the other world, within count noise). In both worlds it flags fewer low readings than today's model in the cooling months (0.845 to 0.898 of today's recall), and it loses reading recall in the 15 C control too (22 against 25 of 44 low readings in the weak-wall world, 27 against 31 of 52 in the other), so it is not only a seasonal effect. It also missed its robustness bar: on every network and in every world it is somewhere more than 10% worse than the better of task 10's model and the bulk-only variant. Its posterior over the wall's response is higher in the bulk-following world than in the weak-wall world in 94% of Net3's fits, but on Net3, measured against the 15 C control, where the data carry no temperature contrast, it moves toward the weak wall and not toward the steep one (6064 K against the control's 6204 K, where the truth is 7441 K; `outputs/chem/wall_posterior_Net3.png`). The grid's rates are read as 20 C values. In cold months that holds the steepest wall laws at the top of the wall-rate grid on Net3, and on Net2, whose rates sit near the grid's lowest values, the bottom of the bulk-rate and wall-rate grids holds the flat wall laws, so Net2's wall posterior and its out-of-season numbers partly reflect the grid's range (checked after the review, `outputs/chem/task10b_grid_edges_Net2.json`). Both were recorded, not changed. How real pipe walls respond to temperature is still the deciding unknown; a year of real grab samples with plant temperatures would settle it.

## Organics, TOC (iteration 4, task 11): tested, stopped at its stop rule, not adopted (`outputs/chem/first_storm_Net3.png`)

Organic matter in the water consumes chlorine, and many surface waters carry more of it after the first winter storms. Today's model absorbs that into the bulk decay rate it refits from the last three months of samples, so a model fitted on a dry late summer cannot know that November's water will use up chlorine faster. Task 11 makes the plant's monthly TOC an input: `residualmap/organics.py::TocSimGP24` (M_TOC) keeps today's 675-member grid as one hypothesis and adds the same grid with every bulk decay rate times TOC / 2.0 mg/L, the TOC-proportional form fitted by AWWARF and by Powell et al. (2000), with its constant absorbed into the calibrated rate; with TOC at 2.0 every month it is today's model exactly. It was tested against bars written down before the first scored run (`docs/iteration3_journal.md`, task 11), on fresh seeds (Net3 32 to 47, Net2 16 to 23), at a fixed 20 C so temperature cannot confound it, against three simulated truths: decay proportional to TOC (the model's own structure, a debug check), EPANET's second-order chlorine-organics kinetics with a limiting concentration (Clark 1998: decay slows as the water ages and the residual responds nonlinearly to the dose), and the same with a low organic demand that runs out. The TOC schedule (1.5 mg/L from May to October, 3.0 in November, the first storms) and the kinetic constants are assumptions, not measurements (`python -m residualmap.organics <net> <seeds>`; numbers from `outputs/chem/summary_organics_<net>.json`).

M_TOC is measurably better than today's model on held-out readings under all three truths on Net3 (rolling RMSE ratios 0.975 to 0.989), and its daily-minimum map is measurably better after the TOC falls: 0.949 to 0.959 times today's map RMSE from April to July, and 0.898 to 0.910 times when January-to-March samples (TOC 2.5) predict July to September (TOC 1.5), on Net3. Not all of that is the TOC. A grid-only control run after the review (the same model told TOC 1.5 every month, so it knows nothing about TOC; `outputs/chem/task11_review_<net>.json`) shows that the readings gains under the first two truths, the falling-month and out-of-season map gains and the first-storm reading gains need the logged TOC. Part of the rest comes from the TOC block's bulk decay rates reaching below today's grid floor of 0.10 per day, where today's fit sits in 60% to 100% of the windows under the second-order truths: about a third of the readings gain and most of the rolling map gain under the low-demand truth, half of the second-order truth's rolling map gain on Net3, and two thirds of its readings gain on Net2. With the TOC at 2.0 every month it is today's model (control: the same posterior in every window; RMSE ratios within 0.001 on Net3 and 0.006 on Net2, where in one seed's falling months the discrepancy GP's hyperparameter search settled differently from last-bit differences). But the first storm, the case it was built for, did not clear its bar: under the second-order truth its November map RMSE is 0.938 times today's (the bar was 0.85), with more of the low junctions found (0.917 against 0.861 of 483 junction-months) at the cost of more false alarms (38 against 27). In December, just after the storm, its map is worse than today's (1.043 times, interval 1.001 to 1.082, on Net3; 1.082 on Net2). Split into bias and spread after the review, the spread of its errors about equals today's and the difference is the low bias both models share (below), which today's model, not knowing December's TOC is higher, partly cancels; it happens under the matched-structure truth too. The stop rule triggered on Net3 on one robust pair: fitted on January to March and asked for July to September, it flags 44 of the 61 low held-out readings where today's model flags 49 (recall ratio 0.898, interval 0.840 to 0.952). The interval lies below 1.0 because the 5 readings come one each from 5 of the 16 seeds and no seed goes the other way. Read after the fact, not tested: both models' maps are biased low in these simulations (about 0.05 to 0.08 mg/L on Net3 and 0.03 on Net2; the best single grid member is unbiased), and so are today's July-to-September readings (mean error -0.017 mg/L against M_TOC's -0.004). M_TOC, told the summer water carries less organic matter, raises its summer predictions toward the truth, and the same fit read at the fitting months' TOC flags exactly today's 49. Under the matched-structure truth the same test loses reading recall too (0.949; 0.881, interval 0.793 to 0.965, with the whole prior on the TOC law), so a correct TOC law can fire this trigger. On Net2 the stop rule triggered on three reading-recall pairs, each one flag apart on 14 to 22 low readings, within count noise. The dose-axis bar also failed on Net3 (0.769 of fits with less than half the posterior at the dose grid's edges, against a 0.90 bar), but today's model has the same share (0.773), so that is the grid's dose axis, not TOC. So TOC is not wired into the pilot path or the app; the plant log still carries a `toc_mgL` column for later. Within a month, the first-order grid fits the second-order truth about as well as the first-order truth (best single member's map RMSE 0.048 mg/L for both on Net3), so the misfit the plan expected in the oldest water did not appear. With temperature the November map was 0.905 times today's (interval 0.813 to 1.023), but that model kept the temperature model's out-of-season weakness, and its first-storm readings (1.133 times today's RMSE) and falling-month map (1.110 times) were worse than today's.

## Honest limitations

- **Structural mismatch is tested, not represented.** The grid covers global demand, roughness and dose errors and the band carries local ones; a closed pipe or a wrong tank volume is absorbed (recall holds, see the stress test) but never identified. Finding *which* pipe is closed from chlorine samples is a different, later problem.
- **The band is honest on average, not at every n.** Snapshot: 0.95 for a 90% band over n = 3–15, but the 50% band over-covers (0.68) — slightly too wide in the middle. Daily minimum: 0.93 on average, narrowing from 0.96 at n=3 to 0.88 at n=15 (0.84 with the straddle-on-daily-minimum rule). Treat `P(daily min < 0.2)` from a straddle-chosen set of 15 samples as slightly over-confident.
- **Recall traded for precision on the 14:00 snapshot.** The better-calibrated posterior shrinks the low junctions' medians toward the grid centre: recall under random samples is about 5% lower than with the iteration-2 grid (precision 10–16 points higher, F1 higher). The max-uncertainty rule lost 20% recall and should not be used.
- **The daily-minimum recall is nearly saturated on Net3.** Any 5 daytime samples find 97% or more of the junctions that go below 0.2 mg/L at night, because a whole tank-fed zone goes low together. Route and rule comparisons on that metric are decided by two hard scenarios out of eight; the 14:00 snapshot (recall 0.74–0.92) and the 0.2 mg/L precision are the discriminating numbers. Larger networks (task 5) should separate the routes more.
- **Synthetic truth.** No real grab-sample data yet. `docs/pilot_protocol.md` is the path to it: what a utility gives us, how taps become junctions, the monthly deliverable, and the hold-out validation (`python -m residualmap.pilot`) with success criteria fixed in advance — including rotating off-route sites, because a fixed route can never validate the map.
- **Grid posterior is coarse** (5×5×3 decay × 3×3 hydraulic × 5 dose). Fine for six parameters with a 20-second cache; MCMC or an emulator if more are added.
- **The discrepancy GP does not scale to 959 junctions at 15 samples.** On ky4 it raises the snapshot RMSE from 0.08 to 0.11 and the straddle rule's to 0.21; the calibrated simulator with its band is the better map there. A rule for when to switch the GP on (n ≥ J/25, or by marginal likelihood) is the next iteration's job. The 675-run grid takes 15 minutes and 62 MB on ky4.
- **Water chemistry is reduced to calibrated bulk and wall decay rates per three-month window, for free chlorine only (iteration 4, tasks 8 to 14, is changing this).** Water age is handled exactly, through EPANET's transport in every simulation. Water temperature and organics (TOC) are not inputs: their effect is absorbed into the bulk and wall decay rates, which are refitted from the last three months of samples, so a model fitted in winter has no way to know that summer water decays faster. Chloraminated systems are not supported. Iteration 4 is adding temperature, TOC and a separate chloramine mode as explicit inputs, each to be tested in simulation against chemistry the model does not assume. So far tasks 8, 9, 10, 10b and 11 are done: the plumbing (`residualmap/chemistry.py`), the saved checks (`python -m residualmap.checks`, 44 of 44 passing in `outputs/chem/checks_report.json`), water age made visible with the wall-versus-water split of the chlorine loss (above), two temperature-aware models, the second of which learns the pipe wall's temperature response, both tested out of season and stopped at their pre-registered stop rules, and a TOC-aware model tested against second-order chlorine-organics chemistry and stopped at its stop rule too, so neither water temperature nor TOC is an input of the default model (above). They leave the Net3, Net2, persistent stress-test and synthetic-pilot outputs unchanged, 50 of 50 files byte-identical (`outputs/chem/baseline_reproduction.json`); ky4 and the initial-level structural variant were not rerun.
- **Temperature is tested, not adopted** (tasks 10 and 10b). In simulation task 10's temperature-aware model gave no measurable gain on held-out readings. Fitted in winter and asked for summer it was worse; fitted in summer and asked for December it had the better map RMSE but lower recall. The main cause is that it assumed pipe-wall decay rises with temperature as steeply as bulk decay. How real walls respond is not known, and the bulk-only variant that did better on Net3 was not the pre-registered model and would also have triggered the stop rule on Net2. Its temperatures (10 to 20 C), the soil curve and the truth's wall response are assumptions; seasonal demand is not simulated; ky4 was not tested (the plan estimates about 5 hours for one bank there; not measured). Task 10b's model, which learns the wall's response, gained on held-out readings in every simulated world but triggered its stop rule in three of four network and world pairs, so it is not adopted either.
- **Organics (TOC) are tested, not adopted** (task 11). In simulation at a fixed 20 C, a model told the plant's monthly TOC predicted held-out readings measurably better than today's, and its map was measurably better after the TOC fell (part of the gain, by a grid-only control after the review, came from its wider bulk-rate grid and not from TOC), but at the first storms (TOC doubling in November, an assumed schedule) its map RMSE was 0.938 of today's (interval 0.874 to 1.016, not a measurable gain) against a pre-registered 0.85, in December its map was worse (the low bias below, which today's TOC-blind error partly cancels), and its stop rule triggered (one robust reading-recall pair on Net3, three within count noise on Net2). The truths' kinetic constants and the TOC schedule are assumptions; one limiting concentration per run assumes one plant dose; ky4 was not tested. Today's daily-minimum map is biased low by about 0.05 mg/L on Net3 and 0.03 on Net2 in these 12-month simulations (and in task 9's), which is conservative for recall and was not changed. Today's bulk-rate grid floor (0.10 per day) binds in most fits under the second-order truths and on Net2; that was not changed either.
- **The water-age range is not called a 90% band** (task 9): it covers the simulated truth's daily-mean age at 0.58 to 0.87 of junctions (0.18 on Net2 with a wrong tank) and the daily-max age at 0.78 to 0.81, because errors in single junctions' demands and single pipes' roughness are not among its 9 global settings. ky4's daily-mean 0.868 would pass the plan's 0.85 bar; the label rule here is stricter. The age weighted by the samples' posterior is closer on average but its band is narrower still. The calibrated split puts less of the loss at the walls than the truth on Net2 (median 0.768 against 0.818) and ky4 (0.538 against 0.624).
- **Every 7-day run carries a start-up transient** (found in task 9, not fixed). Runs start the file's tanks and pipes with no chlorine and with water of age 0, and on the scored day the median junction's water is still 13.7% initial contents on Net2 and 15.9% on ky4 (1.3% on Net3; `outputs/chem/water_age_<net>.json`), with some ky4 junctions reached by no source water in 7 days. So the water ages are lower bounds at most Net2 and ky4 junctions (above), and the truth and the model's grid start alike, so model-against-truth scores are consistent, but absolute counts of low junctions on Net2 and ky4 partly reflect it. A longer warm-up, or tanks started at source quality, is the proposed fix.
- **The route optimiser's edge is Net3-specific so far.** On Net2 and ky4 the K highest-demand sites do as well or better on recall, and the straddle-chosen sites hurt calibration at K=12. A mixed objective (half the sites for the violation call, half to calibrate) is proposed in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## The app (`app.py`)

```bash
streamlit run app.py
```

Pick an example network or upload your `.inp`, set the plant dose, type your grab samples as rows (junction, hour, mg/L), and read four panels: the chlorine map with your samples marked, the 90% band, `P(residual < 0.2)` at the daily minimum or at any hour, and next month's route with a one-sentence reason per site. Below them, the water age of your file with its range across demand and roughness errors (marked "at least" where the 7-day simulation has not replaced the water it started with), and where each junction's chlorine is lost (water or pipe walls) for the single most likely decay rates your samples calibrate; the route reasons and the PDF carry the water age too. Download the one-page PDF. With no samples yet the map is your model's physics alone. Demo mode simulates a hidden truth from the same file, draws daytime samples from it, and can reveal the truth to score the flags. Runs on a laptop; nothing leaves it.
